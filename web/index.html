What is disclosed when submitting?

This direct-verification implementation sends the signed headers and complete signed body as public transaction data. They may contain your address or unsubscribe tokens. The local test samples contain no personal data. A ZK implementation would be needed to hide private email contents.

Published rules, fixed at deployment

Agreement without a price setter

01

Anyone can submit

No reporter allowlist or privileged transaction sender. Each newsletter’s authenticated identity is fixed in the source policy.

02

Exact matching

Two of three publications must report the same month and exact amount. Additional submissions must agree too. Duplicate emails cannot add votes.

03

A fixed closing time

Submissions remain open for 45 days after month end. Anyone can finalize afterward. A missing quorum or any conflict leaves the month unfinalized.

04

Visible dependencies

Cryptography authenticates the publication, not economic truth. Publishers, signing services, DNSSEC’s root and the chain remain dependencies.

Contract and policy details

No upgrade, owner, manual price setter or key override exists in this deployment. The DNSSEC verifier’s configuration is permanently frozen. A production deployment needs genuine source identities, validated templates and the public DNS root anchor.

Supported email formats and limits

RSA-SHA256 (2048–4096 bits, exponent 65537), relaxed/relaxed DKIM with signed timestamp and signed From, Content-Type and Content-Transfer-Encoding. Full body coverage only. Text/plain, text/html, quoted-printable, base64 and bounded multipart messages. Unsupported encodings, duplicate records and ambiguous matches are rejected. Fixed templates currently apply to the three synthetic publications.
