Rules for incomplete or conflicting evidence

Evidence receivedContract behavior
Two or three exactly matching publicationsEligible to finalize after the deadline
Only one publicationNo finalized rate
Any conflicting signed priceMonth blocked, including source equivocation
Another copy from the same publicationNo additional vote
A late email or correctionCannot change a closed month

Submissions close 45 days after month end. A conflicting publication can block liveness; there is no administrator who can resolve it.

The limit of the claim

The remaining trust dependencies

The implementation removes intermediary price-reporting trust. Signed newsletters cannot provide a system with zero trust assumptions.

Publishers and signing services

Their keys authenticate their assertions. A valid signature cannot establish that a rent statistic is true.

DNSSEC, the chain and fixed policy

DNSSEC binds keys to names. Chain consensus executes the rules. The selected sources and measurement remain explicit policy choices.

Anyone can submit under the same rules.
No owner can set a price, replace a key or change this feed’s policy.

The ENS component named “DNSSEC oracle” is used only as a frozen cryptographic proof verifier, not as a price-reporting service.

Limits that affect deployment

Cost and disclosure in direct verification

Measured local test submissions

4.6–5.3m gas

The signatures and parsing execute on-chain. Larger emails and proof chains cost more.

Supported subset: RSA-SHA256, relaxed DKIM, bounded MIME and fixed templates. A 64 KiB body cap does not guarantee affordability.

Signed contents are disclosed

Verification sends the signed bytes to the RPC. A transaction makes those bytes public, potentially including addresses and unsubscribe links.

ZK would be a separate implementation

It could hide email contents and reduce on-chain work. It would still need authenticated publisher keys and the same measurement policy.

No ZK circuit is used or claimed in this implementation. Current costs are not an estimate for a future proof-based design.

External evidence still required

Requirements for a live source feed

01

Original signed newsletter messages

Matching observations across publications, plus enough history to validate each exact template.

02

Verifiable production key chains

A supported DNSSEC chain for each actual DKIM selector. Unsigned DNS gets no trusted fallback.

03

A published deployment policy

Fixed publishers, series, templates and deadline; target-chain costs and a security review before value-bearing use.

These requirements are not yet satisfied. The running local implementation makes the intended state transition concrete and inspectable.
